01 — the base

The latest K3 is the K3 you already have

I checked the Moonshot org directly rather than trusting a search result. moonshotai/Kimi-K3 was created 13 Jun 2026 and last modified 27 Jul 2026 — the weight drop. Since then: nothing. 1,871,575 downloads, 10,621 likes, and no successor anywhere in the org's 19 repos.

The one that looks like news

Souta30903/Kimi-K3.5 exists on Hugging Face, dated 1 Aug. It has 12 downloads and no model card. It is a name squat, not a release. There is no Kimi K3.5.

So everything genuinely new since your builds is downstream work — other people's abliterations, quantisations and prunes of the same 27 July weights. That is what the rest of this page is about.

03 — the cause

Why they spoke gibberish: K3 is already a 4-bit model

This is the finding. Kimi K3's official weights are 1,561 GB for 2.8 trillion parameters. That is 4.46 bits per parameter. K3 was never a 16-bit model you are compressing — it ships compressed, natively MXFP4.

The proof is in Unsloth's own quantisation ladder, where the "lossless" rung gives the game away:

Q8 is the same size as the original and Q4 is only 50 GB smaller. That is what a natively-4-bit model looks like from the outside: there is nothing left to squeeze. Everything you ran — Q2_K at 929 GB and 1,010 GB — sat underneath the precision the weights were trained at.

Then abliteration stacks a second loss on top

Removing refusals is not a filter you toggle. It is the method in Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction — you find the refusal direction and orthogonalise it out of the residual stream. To do that you must dequantise MXFP4 → bf16, modify the tensors, then requantise. Two lossy conversions, landing below native. Your builds paid both.

And a mixture-of-experts amplifies the damage

A dense model spreads quantisation error across every weight in the forward pass, so errors partly average out. K3 routes each token through 16 experts out of 896. There is no averaging — if the 16 experts a token lands on are 1.3-bit noise, that token is noise. Depth compounds it across 93 layers.

That is the mechanism behind "it starts fine then degenerates": each token is a fresh dice roll over 896 experts, so the model doesn't fail uniformly, it falls off a cliff the moment routing hits badly-damaged experts.

04 — the evidence

The A/B that settles it: spend the budget on bits, not experts

Someone ran the controlled experiment. hellohazime/Kimi-K3-REAP-512GB-GGUF published two builds against the same 512 GB memory budget, same calibration corpus, same tooling — differing only in how the budget is spent.

BuildExperts keptAvg bits/weightSizeSWE-Lancer result
REAP640-IQ1_S more experts, fewer bits 640 / 896~1.6441.4 GB 5/8 tasks · $3,500 earned
REAP576-IQ2_XXS fewer experts, more bits 576 / 896~1.9478.5 GB 7/8 tasks · $13,000 earned — including 3 tasks none of their other setups solved

Throwing away 64 more experts to buy 0.3 more bits per weight nearly quadrupled the measured result. The lesson generalises directly to your problem: at a fixed memory budget, bits per weight is the binding constraint, not expert count — which is the exact opposite of what every Q2_K and IQ1 abliteration does.

How much to trust this

It is 8 tasks, self-reported by the person publishing the weights, with no independent replication. Treat the direction as sound — it matches the precision-floor maths above and the MoE mechanism — and treat the 5/8-vs-7/8 magnitude as marketing until someone else runs it.

This is also why REAP (expert pruning) is where the community actually went, rather than deeper quantisation. It drops whole experts an English-and-code deployment rarely routes to, and keeps real precision on the ones it keeps. The download counts follow: 0xTank REAP568 (398.5 GB, 4,447 dl), hellohazime REAP-512GB (2,094 dl), nota-ai Global-Pruned-50 (837.1 GB, 2,082 dl).

Trap I walked into so you don't

ubicloud/Kimi-K3-Pruned-65B is 41.9 GB and looks like the answer — a small K3 that drops straight onto the Beast. Its card says it "retains the first 3 layer(s) of the original 93" and is "intended for pipeline testing rather than production use". It is a test fixture with a model's name. Its 35B sibling is the same. Nothing in the pruned family is a usable small K3.

05 — the rig

The Beast, measured properly — and two corrections

I probed the workstation directly rather than reusing the old figures, and two things came back different from what our notes said.

Correction 1 — there is one GPU in that machine, not two

nvidia-smi returns a single device: RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation, 97,887 MiB (95.6 GB), driver 582.08. No RTX 5090 is present. Our project notes have been carrying "5090 32 GB + RTX PRO 6000" — that is wrong for this box. VRAM is 95.6 GB, not 128 GB. If the 5090 exists and is simply not installed, say so and it changes the cache maths below (not the verdict).

Correction 2 — the RAM upgrade path is closed on this platform

Board is an MSI MPG X870E CARBON WIFI (MS-7E49) with a Ryzen 9 9950X3D — consumer AM5, 4 DIMM slots, SMBIOS-reported maximum 128 GB. Fitted today: 2 × 32 GB Kingston at 4800 MT/s = 61.6 GB visible, 2 slots free.

So the ceiling here is 128 GB of RAM (optimistically 192 GB if the BIOS takes 48 GB DIMMs). You cannot reach the 512 GB that makes this model resident. That is a platform limit, not a budget one — 512 GB needs Threadripper PRO / Xeon W / EPYC / Mac Studio Ultra.

Storage is the one thing that's plentiful

D: is a WD_BLACK SN850X 8 TB with 5,213 GB free — the 579.5 GB download is a non-issue. Measured unbuffered with winsat: sequential read 6,726 MB/s, random 16 K read 428 MB/s. Hold on to that second number; it is what decides everything in §08.

07 — what I'd do

Three options, in the order I'd take them

Don't bother with

  • Any K3 uncensored build on the Beast. 2.5× short at the absolute floor, and the floor is the gibberish zone.
  • Another Q2_K or IQ1 abliteration. Same mechanism, same outcome — you've now tried four.
  • The "pruned 65B/35B" repos. 3-layer test fixtures.
  • Waiting for K3.5. It doesn't exist and nothing suggests it's coming.

Worth doing

  • Test whether uncensored K3 is even worth it before spending a byte — the 24-of-26 number says it may not be.
  • The unfilled gap: nobody has published a REAP-pruned and abliterated build. Prune hard, keep bits high, then ablate — that's the only shape that could ever reach 128 GB.
  • Pick an uncensored model sized for the Beast instead of forcing a 2.8T one into it.

  1. Settle the question for free, on someone else's hardware

    Before any download, find out whether uncensored K3 actually gives you something stock K3 won't. If the honest answer is "two prompts in twenty-six", the whole line of work closes and you've spent nothing. (Search results point at a hosted uncensored K3 endpoint on Venice.ai — I have not verified that myself, so treat it as a lead, not a fact.)

  2. If you still want it local, the floor is a 512 GB machine

    Not the Beast. REAP576-IQ2_XXS class weights, fully resident, at ~3 tok/s — and you'd have to run the abliteration yourself on top, since the pruned-and-uncensored combination doesn't exist yet. That is a real project, not an afternoon.

  3. Or change the model, not the quantisation

    The Beast's 128 GB of VRAM is a lot — it comfortably holds a model whose native precision fits, which is the whole difference between working and babbling. Say the word and I'll pull the current list of uncensored models that fit 128 GB at 4-bit or better, sorted by what they're actually good at.

The one-line rule to carry forward

Never quantise below a model's native precision. Check it first: total bytes ÷ parameter count. If that lands near 4 bits, the model already shipped quantised and every rung below Q4 is destroying weights that have no redundancy left — and on a sparse MoE, that shows up as gibberish rather than as gentle degradation.

08 — the answer

How you would actually run Ryanchen911's build on the Beast

"Doesn't fit in memory" does not mean "won't run". llama.cpp memory-maps the GGUF and pulls pages off disk on demand, so the file can be far larger than RAM. What you buy with that is speed, and here is exactly what it costs.

Why it can run at all: only 104B of the 2.8T parameters are touched per token

That is the whole reason this is even a conversation. At this build's average of 1.66 bits per parameter, one token needs roughly 21.6 GB of weights read — not 579.5 GB. Your 95.6 GB of VRAM plus about 50 GB of usable page cache holds ~145 GB, which is 25% of the model; because expert routing is skewed rather than uniform, the real hit rate lands nearer 35–45%. So each token drags roughly 13 GB off D:.

The download is the easy part

Measured on your rig just now, pulling directly from the actual repo file: 22.7 MB/s from HuggingFace (300 MB in 13.2 s). So 579.5 GB is a 7-hour overnight download, onto a drive with 5,213 GB free. That part is genuinely trivial. (Cloudflare's speed-test endpoint 403s the rig, so this is the HuggingFace number, which is the one that matters anyway.)

The run configuration that makes it as fast as it can be

Three flags do almost all the work here, and getting them wrong is the difference between 0.15 tok/s and not finishing at all:

  • --no-warmup — without it llama.cpp touches every tensor at load, i.e. reads all 579.5 GB before answering anything. That alone is 24 minutes at best and hours at worst.
  • mmap left ON (the default) — never pass --no-mmap, which would try to read the whole file into 61.6 GB of RAM and die.
  • -ngl 99 plus --n-cpu-moe N — this is the real knob. It keeps attention, the F32 router and the shared experts on the GPU while pushing routed experts to mmap. Tune N downward until VRAM sits around 90 of 95.6 GB, so every gigabyte of the card you aren't using for attention is working as an expert cache. Each layer's full expert set is roughly 5.6 GB, so you can hold about 15 layers' worth on the card.
  • -c 8192 — the model advertises 1M context; do not ask for it. KV cache is memory you are taking away from the expert cache, and the expert cache is what sets your speed.
Two risks specific to your box

Windows is the wrong OS for this. A 579.5 GB mmap across 34 shards leans entirely on the page-cache manager, and Windows handles that standby list poorly under pressure — our own H3 work already hit "Windows silently spills VRAM with zero errors" on this same machine. Linux manages a mapping this size considerably better. That is a real variable, not a detail.

It is still the IQ1_S expert class. Ryanchen911 keeps the router at F32 and attention at IQ4_XS and ships an imatrix — those target precisely the failure you hit — but the experts are still ~1.6 bits, which is the exact arm that lost the A/B in §04 by 5/8 to 7/8. This is the best-engineered shot at it, but it is a shot.

And if you install the 5090

Adding it takes VRAM from 95.6 to 127.6 GB, so the cache goes from ~25% to ~31% of the model. That is worth maybe 20–30% more throughput — roughly 0.15 → 0.19 tok/s. Real, but it does not change the category the thing sits in.

So, concretely

Yes, you can run it — as an overnight batch oracle, not as a chat model. Cost is one 7-hour download and a day of setup, $0 in money. You queue questions, it answers 8 or 9 of them by morning, exactly like the render pipeline already works. If what you want is something to talk to, this is the wrong 580 GB and the right move is a model sized for the card.
